# D2412 — Cross-KB links follow a move, a transfer and a delete through the other KB's own tool call

**Decision.** After `concept_move`, `concept_archive` or `concept_transfer` commits, the cross-KB links (`[[kb:<name>/<id>]]`) to the moved pages are retargeted in every other KB the caller can write, by calling that KB's own advanced tool `cross_link_rewrite` through `Deps.CallKB`, one KB at a time in name order, after the origin released its lock. "Can write" is the whole-KB write grant on a KB the connection serves and does not hold read-only. The commit in the other KB carries the fixed reason `cross-KB links updated after a move in another KB`, never the origin's. A delete refuses with the same `inbound_links` error for the pages of KBs the caller can write and sees, and `force` lifts that cross-KB part (on `concept_delete`, and per `delete` operation of `concept_batch`). The rewrite never writes the self-named form `[[kb:<own>/...]]`: a link that lands in the KB holding it becomes `[[<id>]]`, and a transfer fixes its moved pages inside its own target commit. A KB rename is not rewritten: the old name resolves through `kb_aliases`, guarded by the page's KB's relation of that name (an alias is followed only to the relation's remote).

**Why.** A write in another KB must be that KB's own write: its authorization, write veto, audit, lock, write gate, commit and push, as if the caller had called the tool there; no helper writes another KB's files and nothing runs as a system user beyond the caller's scopes. Running the follow-ups outside `gitWrap` and `twoKBMove`, one at a time, means no code of the layer holds two KB locks, so `concept_transfer` needs no further exception to the two-lock rule and no ordering can deadlock with a move in the opposite direction. The follow-up reason is a constant because a `Reason:` trailer in KB X would show text of KB A to everyone who reads X and not A. The write grant is the whole-KB one, the line `concept_move` already draws for rewriting inbound links: a scoped writer cannot be allowed to touch pages outside its scope, so its links dangle and are reported to the KB's governors by lint.

**Alternatives rejected.** Rewriting inside the origin's `gitWrap` or `AfterCommit`: runs under the origin's lock and would hold two locks. A shared helper that writes the other KB's files: skips its policy, hooks, audit and gate. Undoing the origin when another KB fails: breaks the links of every KB already rewritten, and the origin is correct on its own; the result reports `failed` with the retargets and `cross_link_rewrite` (idempotent) is the retry. Refusing the move because of a KB the caller cannot write: would reveal that links exist there. Rewriting links on `kb rename`: the rename is an offline operator action with no caller to bound the writes, and a name belongs to the mount, not to the KB (D772). Turning internal links of a transferred page into cross links to the target: shows the target's name and id to every reader of the source; left as text. Making `force` lift the internal check of `concept_batch`: out of scope; the batch has no cross-KB batch, so a link cycle between two KBs needs a one-call escape only for the cross part.

**Consequences.** A crash between the origin's commit and the follow-ups, or a failed KB, leaves `broken_cross_kb_link` there until `cross_link_rewrite` runs; no journal is kept. Other id-changing paths (`concept_merge`, `kb_repair`, `map_delete`, `concept_expand`/`collapse`, `artifact_move`) do not rewrite cross links, and `kb_repair` never writes into another KB because the layer wraps the registered tools only. The response speaks of readable KBs only, and `TestMoveNonDisclosure` pins that an unreadable KB changes no byte of it; any new field of the report must keep that. The fixed-cost budget of the agent profile stays unchanged: the tool is advanced, and `concept_batch`'s schema grows by one optional field. Whoever adds a write path to another KB goes through `Deps.CallKB` after releasing the lock.
